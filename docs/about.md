## Why This Intelligent Textbook

Hydroponics sits at the intersection of biology, chemistry, engineering,
food science, and data science — and the world needs students fluent in
all five at once. Climate stress, urban food deserts, and agricultural
water scarcity are converging just as inexpensive sensors, LEDs, and
microcontrollers are bringing precision growing within reach of any
classroom. The next generation of farmers will not look like the last,
and the curriculum that prepares them must be built differently.

**In the United States (2024–2025):**

- The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment of agricultural
  and food scientists to grow about **8% from 2023 to 2033**, faster than
  the average for all occupations, with roughly **3,800 openings each year**
  on average[^1].
- USDA reports that **agriculture, food, and related industries
  contributed about $1.5 trillion** to U.S. GDP in 2023 — roughly
  **5.5% of the entire economy**[^2].
- According to NCES, fewer than **half of U.S. 8th-graders** scored
  proficient or above in science on the most recent NAEP assessment,
  underscoring the gap between economic demand and educational
  preparation[^3].

**Worldwide:**

- The UN Food and Agriculture Organization reports that agriculture
  accounts for roughly **72% of all freshwater withdrawals globally** —
  the single largest pressure on the planet's water supply[^4].
- The United Nations projects the world population will reach about
  **9.7 billion by 2050**, requiring a roughly **50% increase in food
  production** over 2012 levels[^5].
- Hydroponic systems can use as little as **10% of the water** required
  by conventional field agriculture for the same crop yield, per peer-
  reviewed comparisons of resource efficiency in controlled-environment
  agriculture[^6].

These numbers are not abstract. They describe the kitchens your students
will cook in, the careers they will compete for, and the planet they
will inherit. Hydroponics is one of the few topics where a curious
fifteen-year-old can build something real in a weekend that demonstrates
a principle being used to feed cities.

This book takes a fundamentally different approach to the subject. It
is built on a **learning graph of 500 interconnected concepts**
organized so that each chapter introduces only what the previous chapter
has prepared the student for — no jumping ahead, no orphan vocabulary.
Throughout the book you will find **30 interactive MicroSims** —
browser-based simulations that let students manipulate nutrient
solutions, tune VPD, model financial returns of a vertical farm, and
diagnose nutrient deficiencies through direct experimentation rather
than memorization. The entire textbook is **open source and free** — no
paywalls, no access codes, no expensive annual editions, and every
diagram, chart, and simulation can be remixed under a Creative Commons
license.

## How to Use This Book

This textbook is designed for self-paced study and classroom use. Each
chapter builds on previous material, so reading in order is recommended.
The book includes:

- **21 Chapters** covering plant physiology, nutrient chemistry, system
  design, lighting and environmental control, MicroPython and sensor
  hardware, IoT and data visualization, food safety, solar energy,
  vertical farming, and financial modeling
- **30 Interactive MicroSims** embedded in chapters — browser-based
  simulations you can manipulate to explore concepts (root anatomy,
  Mulder's chart, VPD dashboard, PPFD/DLI calculator, financial models,
  and more)
- **Chapter Quizzes** at the end of each chapter to test understanding
- **Annotated References** linking to Wikipedia and authoritative sources
- **Glossary** with precise definitions for every key concept
- **Learning Graph** visualizing how 500 concepts connect across chapters
- **Search** available from any page using the search bar (top right)

The [Learning Graph](learning-graph/index.md) visualizes how concepts
connect across chapters. If you want to explore non-linearly or check
prerequisites for a specific topic, start there.

## About the Author

Dan McCreary is a semi-retired AI researcher, solution architect, and
educator who has spent more than three decades helping Fortune 100
organizations reason over massive datasets. At Optum he founded the
Generative AI Center of Excellence and led the team that built one of
the world's largest healthcare knowledge graphs — spanning over 25
billion vertices — to unify member, provider, and patient insights.

used throughout this course.

## How to Cite This Book

If you reference this textbook in academic work, curriculum proposals,
lesson plans, or other publications, please use one of the following
citation formats.

**APA (7th edition)**

McCreary, D. (2026). *Hydroponics: From Mason Jar to Vertical Farm*. https://dmccreary.github.io/hydroponics/

**Chicago (17th edition)**

McCreary, Dan. 2026. *Hydroponics: From Mason Jar to Vertical Farm*. https://dmccreary.github.io/hydroponics/.

**MLA (9th edition)**

McCreary, Dan. *Hydroponics: From Mason Jar to Vertical Farm*. 2026, dmccreary.github.io/hydroponics/.

**BibTeX**

```bibtex
@book{mccreary2026hydroponics,
  title     = {Hydroponics: From Mason Jar to Vertical Farm},
  author    = {McCreary, Dan},
  year      = {2026},
  url       = {https://dmccreary.github.io/hydroponics/},
  note      = {Interactive intelligent textbook}
```

To cite a specific chapter, append the chapter number and title — for
example:
